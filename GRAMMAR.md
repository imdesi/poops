
In grammar, these are past-oriented verb tenses that, according to the linguistic framework referenced in the text, help characterize objective narrative:

- **Preterit:** Often referred to as the simple past tense, it describes a completed action that occurred at a specific point in the past (for example, "she walked" or "he conquered"). In many languages like French, this is the *passé simple*, which is traditionally reserved for storytelling and literature rather than spoken conversation.
- **Pluperfect:** Also known as the past perfect, this tense indicates an action that was completed before another past action or point in time (such as "she had walked" or "he had conquered").

As the author outlines through the linguist Émile Benveniste, strict narrative relies on the third person alongside forms like the [preterit and the pluperfect](https://read.readwise.io/read/01m269rr8a5b940hs6k1aw8kvz?endLexposition=00000_0000p_00000-008z&startLexposition=00000_0000p&emphasizeText=preterit+and+the+pluperfect) to create an objective account where events appear to unfold on their own, completely removed from an active speaker's present.
