The Street Committee to whom was referred the Petitions to have the Triangular piece of ground in the 6th Ward called Five Points opened presented the following Report in favor of the same. The Street Committee to whom was referred the Petition of a large and respectable number of Citizens to have the Triangular piece of Ground in the Sixth Ward Bounded by Anthony Cross and Little Water streets and Known as part of the place called the "Five Points" opened Respectfully Report [37] That your Committee have examined the premises and duly considered the subject, and as but one objection has been made thereto are of the opinion that the request of the Petitioners ought to be granted they find that nearly all the buildings occupying the ground are in a ruinous Condition of but Little value and occupied by the lowest description and most degraded and abandoned of the human Species. Enormities of every Kind (of which the public are but too well informed) are daily committed in that sink of iniquity, the which your Committee forbear to particularise. To remedy which in part, if not wholly to remove the existing evil, they propose the following Method Viz* That the whole of the Triangle be taken by Commissioners of Esti mate and Assessment, that so much thereof as may be necessary to make Cross Street and Little Water Street each 40 feet wide to be assessed on persons deemed to be benefitted and the remainder of the ground to be appropriated for Public purposes either to the erection of buildings or such other manner as future exigencies may require. Your Committee in accordance with the foregoing Report (and forasmuch as they have received but one solitary objection thereto) submit the following Resolutions. [38] Resolved that the Triangular piece of ground in the Sixth ward Bounded by Anthony Cross, and Little water Streets be opened and appropriated in the manner expressed in the foregoing Report. Resolved that the Counsel to this Board adopt the necessary meas ures for having the foregoing Resolution carried into effect.

Environmental determinism and the city: a historical-cultural note

Author(s): Yang

A superb building can make one feel young. This is environmental determinism in

its purest form. But, then, a kind word from the waitress in an all-night restaurant can

also make one feel young. And political wisdom delivered by a loquacious but friendly

cabdriver can lift the spirit. Determinisms and influences, exerted by weather,

topography, buildings and people, are everywhere. However, this fact does not in the

least mean that human passivity is all pervasive. We human beings, after all, make the

buildings; we take the initiative in partitioning space; and we speak. In a large

American city, even while concrete blocks and graffiti-covered tenement walls oppress

and depress, one cannot altogether forget their human provenance, their human

ingenuity, and their assertions of power for both moral and immoral purposes; and one cannot altogether ignore, even in a run-down street or bland shopping mall, the

myriad small, easily overlooked efforts to communicate, sustain, and achieve.
